Overview

Internal Family Systems (IFS) is a fast-growing therapy modality that treats the mind as a system of distinct parts — managers, firefighters, exiles — each with its own role and protection logic.

All My Parts is a journaling app shaped around IFS. Each entry tags which parts showed up. An AI summary layer reads across entries to surface themes a person might otherwise miss — which parts are most active, what triggers them, where Self-energy is breaking through.

The problem

Therapists routinely ask IFS clients to track parts between sessions. Most clients do it on paper or in a Notes app. Both work, but neither makes patterns visible across weeks of entries.

Existing journaling apps assume a single self journaling alone. IFS's central premise — that the mind is plural — breaks that assumption. The data model needs to know about parts.

research artifact — IFS workflow today

What I built

A web app where each journal entry can be tagged with the parts that showed up — manager, firefighter, exile — and the Self-energy felt in the moment. Over time, the parts library becomes the user's personal map.

An AI summary layer reads across recent entries and surfaces themes weekly: which parts are most active, what triggers them, where Self-energy is showing up. The goal isn't to replace the therapist — it's to give the client a clearer hand-off into the next session.

Stack & approach

Rails on the backend with the OpenAI API for the summary layer. Stripe for billing, Google Auth for SSO. The hard work wasn't the AI — it was the data model and the language. Calling a "manager" a "manager" and a "firefighter" a "firefighter" without sounding clinical took several rewrites.

Built solo over a few months using Cursor, on Rails — chosen because the LLM-meets-CRUD shape of the app maps cleanly to convention-over-configuration.

Where it is now

In paid beta at allmyparts.me — $12.99/month or $99/year. Early users skew toward people already in IFS therapy who use it between sessions, plus IFS-curious people building a self-practice.

Next: build the credibility scaffolding (therapist endorsements, a small Reddit and substack presence) before pushing on paid acquisition. Reddit-organic + cold-email to IFS therapists have both been tried and failed; the path forward needs a credibility-first approach.

02 — The shift

The build got cheap. The yes didn't.

Anyone can ship a prototype now. The hard part was never the code — it's making something people actually get excited about, fund, and come back to. That's a design problem, and it's the one that decides whether your idea becomes a company.

Most people you'd hire for this are career designers. I'm a designer who's also shipped and run my own products — priced them, watched the signup graph stay flat, killed the features nobody used, felt every consequence of getting the experience wrong.

So I don't hand you a beautiful Figma file and walk away. I design for the outcome you actually need — board sign-off, the investment commitment, a user who comes back — because I've had to earn those exact yeses with my own money on the line.

Engineers come at it from the backend. PMs come at it from the spec. I come at it from the experience — the part that gets people to say yes. The backend gets rebuilt anyway.

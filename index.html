03 · The partnership

Embedded, not outsourced.

Two days a week, in your Slack and your standups, working on whatever is most in the way that week. Some weeks that's the onboarding flow. Some weeks it's telling you the feature you're excited about isn't the one to build.

You get direction and the actual work: core flows, the design system, the front end when writing it is faster than explaining it. Nothing waits on a handoff, because there isn't one.

05 · Why this, now

Production is solved. Judgment isn't.

You can ship a working product in a weekend now. That used to be the hard part, and it isn't anymore. What's left is the part the tools can't do: deciding what deserves to exist, what to cut, and why the thing you just shipped feels almost right but not quite.

Most people you'd hire for this are career designers. I'm a designer who has also shipped and run my own products, priced them, watched the signup graph stay flat, killed the features nobody used, felt every consequence of getting the experience wrong.

That's why I don't hand you a Figma file and walk away. I sit inside the work until the product earns the outcome you need: board sign-off, the raise, a user who comes back. I've had to earn those exact yeses with my own money on the line.

Engineers come at it from the backend. PMs from the spec. I come at it from the experience, the part that gets people to say yes.

08 · About

I'm Humayun, a product designer and builder based in Montreal, working remotely since 2020. Shopify, ClassDojo, and a string of healthcare and AI startups before going independent.

Now I run Studio Maya: I embed with founders as their design partner, and I build my own products. The name is from Raymond Loewy's principle, Most Advanced Yet Acceptable, the line between novel and familiar, which is exactly where good products live.

The studio is one person on purpose. One founder at a time, so the person you meet on the first call is the person in your product every week.

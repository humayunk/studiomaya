01Nothing The control. Type and proof only. If none of the five below beat this, the answer is that the headline should carry the hero alone.
02Sheet furniture only Two registration marks and a drawing title block. No marks touch the type. Quietest option: it says "this is a working drawing" and nothing else. Entirely static, nothing to maintain.
03Leader callout Patent reference-numeral convention: a dot on the word, a leader routed into the margin, a figure label. Plus one red stroke under the word the page is about. The version I showed you before.
04True dimensions Dimension lines measuring the headline's actual cap-height and width, labelled with real computed pixels. Drag the window and the numbers change. The only treatment where the annotation is literally true rather than decorative.
05Keyed to a spec list Extension lines drop from three words into a numbered key below. Most information, most risk: nine annotation elements on one headline is right at the noise ceiling.
06Hover to measure Invisible at rest. Hover the hero and a Figma-style redline overlay appears with real px values. Costs nothing until someone is curious, and every designer or founder who has used Figma decodes it instantly. Hover this one.

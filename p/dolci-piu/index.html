01 · Where things stand

Customers can't easily reach you, and when they do, their messages land in two places that don't talk to each other. Here's what I heard on October 13, plus what I found looking up dolcipiu.ca:

  • Two inboxes. The Bell address (@bellnet.ca) is the 22-year-old one that suppliers, regulars, CEGEPs and hospitals still use. info@dolcipiu.ca is the newer one. Info forwards to Bell, but big photos and Interac e-transfers don't make it, so the team checks both, plus Roundcube, which keeps logging you out.
  • Lost attachments. Replies don't keep the original photo or file, so you dig through the "follow-ups" and "follow-ups not booked" folders to find it.

  • The website is old. It's a WordPress site, already in French and English, but it has no catering menu or gallery, and the forms don't ask for a phone number, servings or inspiration photos. That's where the back-and-forth comes from.
  • Christmas is coming. 500 bûches across 5 or 6 flavours, and regulars start ordering in early December.

02 · Part 1

One inbox, and a domain you own.

Everything sent to either address shows up in one place, every attachment arrives, and you reply from info@ so Bell fades out over the next year.

The good news: we don't need to take over Mary's Bluehost account. We only need the domain name. Once Dolci Più owns the name, it decides where the email and website live, and we point both somewhere new. Mary's part takes her about five minutes.

Step by step

  1. You open a free GoDaddy account in Dolci Più's name. It goes under Shauna's own email, not info@, so you never get locked out while the email is moving. Two-step login goes on Shauna's phone. I'll sit with you for the 10 minutes it takes.
  2. Mary moves the domain into that account. GoDaddy has a built-in "move to another GoDaddy account" option. She enters your customer number, you accept, and it's done. Nothing goes offline, because the website and email keep running at Bluehost.
  3. The legal owner gets updated. For a .ca name, the owner on record should be the business itself. I update it to Dolci Più's legal name, and Shauna clicks to accept the registry's agreement by email. Auto-renew goes on.
  4. Copy the settings, then take over the controls. Today the domain's settings (where the website and email live) sit inside Mary's Bluehost. I copy them exactly into your GoDaddy account and switch over to that copy. Customers notice nothing, but now you hold the controls.
  5. Set up Google Workspace for info@dolcipiu.ca. That's Gmail with your own address, one shared login to start. It works in a browser, in the Mail app you already use, on the office computer and on phones, and never logs you out.
  6. Bring over your old mail. You already have the Roundcube password, so I copy everything from Bluehost into Gmail, including the follow-ups folders.
  7. Switch email on a Sunday night. Then I test it: a large photo, an e-transfer notification and a form submission all have to arrive. Your Interac autodeposit keeps working because the address doesn't change.
  8. Bring Bell into the same screen. Bell forwards to info@, and both accounts sit in the Mail app on the office computer as a backup, in case a forward fails. Replies go out from info@.
  9. Automatic replies. Bell answers every message with "we've moved, please use info@dolcipiu.ca." Info@ confirms receipt and lists what a cake request needs: date, number of servings, phone number and a photo.
  10. Mary closes the old hosting. Once the new website is live (Part 2), Mary can remove the old site and mailbox from her Bluehost. We keep a full copy first.
  11. Walkthrough and checklist. 30 minutes with the team, Gmail labels to replace the follow-ups folders, and a list of places to update: YouEats, Too Good To Go, Google listing, suppliers, invoice footers.

What I need from Mary: one action at GoDaddy (step 2) and, at the end, closing the old hosting (step 10). I'll write her a short email with the exact steps so it's easy for her to say yes.

What's on the site

  • Shauna's design, polished, and easy to read on a phone.
  • Cake gallery by category. Wedding, birthday, baby shower, vintage, floral and so on.
  • Catering menu, for the first time, with combos (choose a main, choose your sides) so people know what to order.
  • Custom desserts. Cupcakes, cake pops, éclairs, profiterole and macaron towers.
  • Holiday page. This year's bûches with photos, flavours, sizes and prices.
  • Order online button. Links to YouEats today. When you switch systems, it's a one-line change.
  • French and English. Quebec law requires a French version of a business website. Your current site already has French text, so I'll carry it over and update it. Someone on your team reviews anything new.

Your admin page

Log in at dolcipiu.ca/admin to add cake photos to a gallery category, change catering items and prices, and update the holiday page. Each item has its French and English text side by side.

Three forms, built around the questions you always end up asking

  1. Custom cake: date, servings, style, buttercream or fondant, tiers, budget, inspiration photos. Phone and email are required, so no order is missing a way to reach the customer.
  2. Custom desserts: a shorter version for cupcakes, towers and the rest.
  3. Catering: date, headcount, combos and dietary needs.

Every submission lands in the new info@ inbox with the photos attached. The customer automatically gets a confirmation, in their language, saying what happens next. Customers who'd rather email still can, and the auto-reply from Part 1 tells them what to include.

In-store tablet: the cake form runs full screen on a tablet at the counter, so walk-ins fill it out themselves instead of on paper slips.

Handover: a 45-minute session on the admin page, plus a short screen recording to refer back to.

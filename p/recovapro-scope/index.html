01 · Where things stand

Three rules, and the plan follows each one.

  • The person in pain comes first. They recognise their story before a GP or physio sees it.
  • Every line ties back to what they said. The summary keeps their words, and a clinician can check where each line came from.
  • Consent and data handling are agreed first. Nobody is recruited and no customer information is shared until you've signed off on how it's handled.

You also said the summary is one step towards helping people understand their pain and find what helps, not the whole product. So this piece tests that one step. It is as small as it can be while still answering both questions: do people recognise themselves, and does a clinician get something useful from it?

02 · Part 1

Set up, before anyone is recruited.

You sign off on everything a customer or clinician will see, and every rule for their data, before a single invite goes out.

  1. Kickoff call. We agree who to invite first (customers with everyday back, neck or shoulder pain), what counts as someone recognising their story, and what a clinician has to get from the summary for it to be useful.
  2. The consent pack. A one-page information sheet and consent form for customers, and a shorter one for clinicians, in plain English. You and Dr Bonfanti review both.
  3. The data paperwork. A data processing agreement between Recovapro and Studio Maya, a short data protection impact assessment, and a one-page data handling plan (section 05).
  4. The story prompts. The questions for each call, built from RecovaSmart's own: where it hurts, what it feels like, what it stops you doing, what you've tried, and what you'd want a clinician to understand. Plus a short safety script for the start of every call.
  5. The invitation and sign-up form. A plain research invitation you send from your own email to your own customers, with no sales content, and a short form for people who want to take part. I never receive your customer list.
  6. The two-view summary, first version. A clickable page built from a made-up story. One view gives the person's story in their own words. The other is structured for a clinician to scan, with each line linked back to the words it came from. You see it before anyone else does.

What could go wrong: recruiting before the paperwork is ready. So nothing goes to customers until you've signed off steps 2 to 5.

03 · Part 2

Round 1: five customers, three clinicians.

Five customers tell their story, check their summary, and decide whether a clinician sees it. Then three clinicians react.

  1. Invite and choose. You send the invitation. From the people who reply, we choose five with a mix of pain, age and how long they've lived with it. Each signs the consent form before their call.
  2. Story calls. A video call of about 45 minutes each, recorded for transcription only. They talk, I ask the prompts and follow their lead.
  3. Summaries, drafted then checked. I remove names and identifying details from each transcript, draft the summary with AI, then check every line against what they actually said.
  4. The recognition test. Each person sees their own summary on the clickable page, in a short follow-up call or on their own if they prefer. They mark what's right, what's wrong, what's missing, and anything they wouldn't share. The bar: they'd share it without major changes.
  5. Their decision. Then a separate yes or no: can a clinician see it, without their name? A no changes nothing about their thank-you.
  6. Clinician sessions. Three clinicians each see two or three of the summaries people agreed to share, in a 30-minute call. Dr Bonfanti first, then a GP and a physio. I ask what they'd do with it, what they'd trust, what they'd check and what's missing.
  7. Findings and readout. A short findings report and a readout call. It covers what people recognised, what they held back, what clinicians used, and the changes to make. It ends with a clear recommendation: run Round 2, change course, or start designing it into RecovaSmart.

05 · Consent and health data

You stay in control of the data.

Every person consents twice, and nothing identifies anyone outside your own folder. This is the one-page plan you sign off in Part 1.

QuestionHow it's handled
Who's responsibleRecovapro is the controller: they're your customers and it's your product. Studio Maya is your processor and only handles their data on your written instructions. UK GDPR requires a data processing agreement for that, and we sign it before recruiting starts.
Legal basisHealth information is special category data, so we rely on each person's explicit consent: a clear written statement that names the health information involved.
Consent, in two stepsFirst to the story call, the recording and the AI drafting. Later, after they've approved their summary, a separate yes or no to a clinician seeing it. They can skip any question and withdraw at any time.
RecruitmentYou email your own customers. I only see the details of people who sign up. Nobody under 18, and nobody currently being treated for cancer in this round.
What we collectFirst name, email, age range and their story. No NHS number, medical records, address or date of birth.
Where it's keptA private folder in Recovapro's Microsoft 365, shared only with you and me, both with two-step login. No email attachments, and nothing kept on personal devices.
Names removedEach person gets a code (P1 to P5). Names, places and other identifying details come out of the transcript before anything else happens. Clinicians only see coded summaries.
AI draftingClaude, through Anthropic's business API, working from coded transcripts only. Anthropic's terms bar it from training on this data, and it deletes inputs and outputs within 30 days. The consent form names it.
Outside the UKI work from Canada, which UK law treats as adequate for businesses covered by Canada's privacy law, as Studio Maya is. Anthropic is in the US, and its data processing terms include the UK's international transfer addendum.
How long it's keptRecordings are deleted once the transcript is checked, within 14 days of each call. Contact details are deleted once thank-yous are sent. Coded transcripts and summaries are deleted 30 days after the readout, unless a person agreed you could keep their anonymous summary.
CliniciansThey see coded summaries only, and only the ones people agreed to share. They give professional feedback, not care, and agree to keep what they see confidential.
SafetyEvery invite, form and call says this is not medical advice. If someone describes urgent symptoms, such as new numbness or loss of bladder or bowel control, or is in distress, I pause and point them to NHS 111, their GP, 999 or Samaritans on 116 123.
NHS ethicsThis is product research with your own customers: nobody is recruited through the NHS and nothing goes into an NHS record. I'll run it through the Health Research Authority's decision tools to confirm NHS ethics review doesn't apply, and keep the result on file.

07 · Not in this phase

Each depends on the summary holding up first.

  • Round 2 and the real hand-off (Optional Part 3). Decided at the Round 1 readout.
  • Cancer-related pain. Higher stakes and different clinicians. Worth testing with a clinical partner once the format works for everyday pain.
  • The rest of RecovaSmart: the body map, pain alongside daily life, and the possibilities that may help. Next, once the summary works.
  • A medical device check. Before RecovaSmart suggests what may help, check whether that makes it a medical device under MHRA rules.
  • Building it into the app. Designing and building the summary into RecovaSmart once the format is proven. That fits a monthly arrangement better than a fixed project, and we can talk about it at the readout.
